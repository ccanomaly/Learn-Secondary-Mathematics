What Is An Exponent?

An exponent is used to denote repeated multiplication. Below is the general form of an exponential expression.
$a^n$
$a$: Base
$n$: Exponent

What does this mean mathematically?

An explanation of exponent is best show by example. Let's take the expression $5\cdot5$. We see that 5 is being multiplied by itself 2 times, so we can then say $5\=5^2$. The exponent signifies the amount of times the base is going to multiply itself by. In the case of $5^2$, we see that it is the base of 5 being multiplied by itself 2 times.

We can then see that the general expression $a^n$ means the base, $a$, being multiplied by itself by $n$ amount of times where $a$ is the base and $n$ is the exponent.

To rewrite this expression as an exponential expression, we first recognize there is repeated multiplication of the same number, 2. We then count how many times 2 is multiplied. In this case, it is 4 times. Lastly, we recognize 2 as the base of an exponential expression where 4 is the exponent since 2 is multiplied by itself 4 times. We now then can rewrite the expression as $2^4$.

Power Terminology

The conventional way to to express an exponential expression $(a^n)$ out loud involves referring to the expression as "the base $(a)$ to the power of the exponent $(n)$." For example, $2^4$ would be expressed as "2 to the power of 4."

What does it mean to have a number to the power of zero?

Let us look at some powers of 3 to see if we can figure out what $3^0$ means.

$3^3 = 3*3*3$
$3^2 = 3*3$
$3^1 = 3$
$3^0 =$ ?

Take a second to see if there is some sort of pattern as you descend powers. Below is how we derive $3^0$.

$3^3 = \underbrace{3*3*\cancel{3}}_{\text{divide by 3 }}$
$3^2=\underbrace{3*\cancel{3}}_{\text{divide by 3}}$
$3^1=\underbrace{\cancel{3}}_{\text{divide by 3}}$
$3^0=1$

We see that if we keep dividing by three one a time, the exponent decreases by 1. This logic follows even when the exponent is 1, meaning when we divide $3^1$ by three, we get three $3^0$. And since $3^1=3$ and $3/3=1$, it then follows that $3^0 = 1$.

This explanation works for any real number! If we let $a$ represent any real number (think of it as a placeholder/variable that we can plug any real number into), we then follow the same logic as before.

$a^3 = \underbrace{a*a*\cancel{a}}_{\text{divide by a }}$
$a^2=\underbrace{a*\cancel{a}}_{\text{divide by a}}$
$a^1=\underbrace{\cancel{a}}_{\text{divide by a}}$
$a^0=1$

$a$ divided by $a$ is one since anything divided by itself is one, therefore any number to the power of zero is one!
